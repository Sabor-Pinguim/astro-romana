# Pilot control panel (Orion layout)

The first-person panel of the cockpit phase (`assets/models/rocket/cockpit_pilot.glb`). It follows the layout NASA describes for the Orion crew module: three display units, seven switch interface panels with about 60 switches, rotational and translational hand controllers and cursor control devices, with physical controls kept for critical actions. A few controls the flight minigame needs (throttle, abort handle, guarded stage and landing switches) are game choices and are marked as such.

**Why tier 2:** a glass cockpit with physical backups, between a bare capsule panel (tier 1) and a full Shuttle-class flight deck (tier 3).

## 4. Low-poly model

Generated by `tools/modeling/parts/cockpit.mjs` (`node tools/modeling/build-parts.mjs cockpit-pilot`), which also writes the copy `assets/models/rocket/cockpit_pilot.glb` used by `index.html`.

### 4.1 Shape in words

From the pilot seat: a dark grey panel leaning away, with three display units in a row, each with keys along the bottom and both sides. Under each display, a light grey switch panel with two rows of six toggles and three green lights. A dark brow tops the panel, with the red master alarm and the amber caution light over the centre display. Two side wings turn toward the pilot, each with a switch panel; the left one also holds 18 circuit breakers. In front of the seat, a pedestal carries the hand controllers, the throttle, two guarded switches and a striped abort T-handle. Two forward windows sit in the slanted bulkhead above, and the cabin walls lean in like the inside of a cone. The armrests carry the cursor controls. Screen graphics (attitude ring, system bars, ascent path) are game art.

### 4.5 Simplify

- Game choices: one pilot station centred on the camera (Orion has two seats with a hand-controller pair each, on the armrests); the hand controllers sit on a pedestal so they are in view; the throttle lever is a minigame control (Orion's crew does not throttle an engine); the abort T-handle follows Crew Dragon's abort handle ([4], via crew-cabins.md); the guarded switches and the screen graphics are game art.
- No labels, logos or text on the panel.

## 6. Gaps and cautions

- All panel dimensions are estimates; a photo or drawing of the Orion display panel would let us check proportions.
- Real Orion crew lie on their backs during launch (`in-context-2.jpg`); the game camera is upright for readability.
- The switch count "about 60" is approximate in the sources [1][2].
